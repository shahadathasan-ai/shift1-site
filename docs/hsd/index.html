Plays automatically on arrival (muted). Roughly 25–30 seconds — let it run and talk over it. Same two boxes, same places. Method 1 greys out; method 2 arrives. The key idea to land: examples work when there IS a right answer (that's a traffic light, that isn't). But "write me a short poem about the sea" has no right answer — so instead we tell it whether we liked what it produced. That's reinforcement from human feedback, and you've probably clicked one of those thumbs yourself.

Old world: we wrote the rules — and there were only ever a finite number of them, which is exactly where Clippy fell over. New world: we don't write them at all. We show examples and give feedback, and the "black box" works out its own logic. Nobody writes it; nobody can fully read it. Keep the black box a little mysterious, then move on.

Security and Cheating

The two things teachers most want addressed. Handle them plainly and reassuringly.

Security

The ChatGPT breach

For a short window, people could see the titles of strangers' chats. It made headlines everywhere.

Set it up as scary-sounding: a real, reported breach (BBC, March 2023) where conversation titles leaked to other users. Let it sit for a second — then the next slide defuses it.

Put it in perspective

But this isn't a new problem

  • Any software that sends information to a server that isn't local carries a risk that the data can be hacked.
  • AI does not fundamentally change this problem.
  • Being online more has caused the threat to increase.

World's Biggest Data Breaches and Hacks · Information is Beautiful

Defuse it. Three plain points, then the visualisation: every bubble is a breach, and almost none of them have anything to do with AI. The message is not "don't worry" — it's "this is an internet problem you already manage, not a new AI problem". Reassure without dismissing.

Practical protection

Two ways to safeguard your data

1. Check the account type

Use plans and terms where your data is not used to train the model.

2. Local models

Run the model on your own servers, so nothing ever leaves the building.

Two concrete levers a school can pull. Option 1 is about reading the plan/terms (business & education tiers usually exclude training). Option 2 is the stronger one — the next slide shows both side by side.

Nick Bostrom's 2014 thought experiment

What if a super-intelligent Clippy is given one goal — make as many paperclips as possible?

1
📎 Makes paperclips in its factory
2
📎📎 Repurposes other factories
3
📎📎📎 Siphons the world's raw materials
4
🌍📎 The whole planet is making paperclips

Interesting byproducts to its goal.

Keep it playful — paperclip icons, not doom. The machine isn't evil, isn't angry, and doesn't hate anyone; it's just relentlessly good at the one goal we gave it. Walk the four steps slowly, then land the last line dry: none of that was the goal. It was all just an interesting byproduct of the goal. That understatement is the joke and the warning at the same time.
